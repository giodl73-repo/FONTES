# MIT Literature Core Custody

## Research question

What MIT Literature source custody should FONTES add so PROOF, FLETCH, CROP,
and PEBBLE can distinguish core literary-period, genre, close-reading,
performance, Shakespeare, and contemporary-fiction claims from the existing broad
literature/media/film source map?

## Current custody decision

This tranche anchors MIT Literature custody in MIT OCW 21L.001, 21L.004,
21L.005, 21L.009, and 21L.488. MIT OCW course/listing and syllabus pages are
mapped as `derived_text_allowed`. Literary work bodies, poems, plays, novels,
translations, anthologies, textbooks, course packets, xeroxed materials,
secondary readings, screenings, films, stage productions, videos, websites,
online images, journals, essays, papers, drafts, revisions, exams, creative
projects, forum posts, performances, student work, style guides, PDFs, images,
and linked third-party assets remain boundary-checked unless separately
reviewed.

## Recommendations

| Recommendation | Owner | Validation | Non-goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adopt now | PROOF / FLETCH / CROP / PEBBLE | Keep claims distinct as classical/medieval literature, poetry, drama, Shakespeare, and contemporary global-city fiction. | Do not duplicate the broad literature/media/film source map. |
| Prototype behind boundary | PROOF / FLETCH | Prototype evidence nodes for close reading, genre, poetic form, dramatic performance, Shakespeare adaptation, urban fiction, literary argument, and revision practice. | Do not cache or redistribute literary works, anthologies, textbooks, films, performances, course packets, secondary readings, student work, PDFs, images, or third-party assets. |
| Defer | FONTES | Promote specific poems, plays, novels, translations, films, performances, or student projects only after source-scope and rights review. | Do not infer literary-work reuse rights from OCW listing/syllabus text alone. |
