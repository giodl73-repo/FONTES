# MIT Brain and Cognitive Sciences Core Custody

## Research question

What MIT-major Brain and Cognitive Sciences source custody should FONTES add so
PROOF, FLETCH, CROP, and PEBBLE can distinguish introductory neuroscience,
human cognitive neuroscience, functional neuroanatomy, animal behavior, and
neural computation claims from the existing broad psychology/learning source
map?

## Current custody decision

This tranche anchors BCS core custody in MIT OCW 9.01, 9.13, 9.14, 9.20, and
9.40. MIT OCW course/listing and syllabus pages are mapped as
`derived_text_allowed`. Textbook bodies, journal articles, lecture notes,
lecture videos, lecture audio, podcasts, problem sets, homework, exams,
solutions, quizzes, student papers, experimental-design assignments, project
reports, MATLAB/code files, lab/dissection materials, images, datasets, brain
talks, selected videos, readings, and linked third-party assets remain
boundary-checked unless separately reviewed.

## Recommendations

| Recommendation | Owner | Validation | Non-goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adopt now | PROOF / FLETCH / CROP / PEBBLE | Keep claims separated as introductory neuroscience, human brain/cognitive neuroscience, functional neuroanatomy, animal behavior, and neural computation. | Do not duplicate the existing broad psychology/learning source map. |
| Prototype behind boundary | PROOF / FLETCH | Prototype evidence nodes for sensory systems, movement, learning/memory, cognitive domains, cortical networks, neuroanatomical structures, ethology/sociobiology, sensory coding, and quantitative neural models. | Do not cache or redistribute readings, notes, videos, audio, podcasts, problem sets, exams, solutions, code, lab/dissection assets, student work, or third-party content. |
| Defer | FONTES | Promote experiment details, brain images, datasets, student designs, software, or biological/lab protocols only after source-scope, privacy, safety, and reuse review. | Do not infer medical, diagnostic, animal-care, or lab-procedure guidance from listing text alone. |
